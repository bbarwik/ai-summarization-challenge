## CORE GOVERNANCE (Questions 1-7)

### 1. Centralized Token?

The protocol is classified as centrally controlled due to the concentration of administrative powers in the 7-of-10 multisig and the delegated Risk Committee model, with ENA holders' influence limited to bi-annual elections. This centralization is evident in the hybrid CeDeFi (centralized-decentralized finance) architecture, where on-chain token ownership achieves independence from traditional banking but stability relies on off-chain delta-hedging executed on centralized exchanges. The circulating supply of USDe stands at approximately 9.65 billion tokens as of October 2025, with the total supply also at approximately 9.65 billion, minted on a 1:1 basis against deposited collateral, and a market capitalization of approximately $9.64 billion closely tracking the $1.00 peg. The 24-hour trading volume is approximately $335 million across exchanges, supporting arbitrage for peg maintenance. The total value locked in the protocol is $9.829 billion as of October 2025, reflecting rapid growth since the public launch on February 19, 2024, and placing it among the largest stablecoin protocols while highlighting strong market adoption in high-yield DeFi synthetics. The circulating supply of ENA is 7,156,250,000 as of October 30, 2025, with a total of 80,702 holders as of October 26, 2025. DAO metrics remain largely inaccessible without direct API access, with estimates suggesting 5 substantive proposals indicating medium activity, stable voter participation with ±10% variation, and unverified token concentration. The overall governance quality is assessed as medium, effectively balancing operational efficiency against centralization risks posed by multisig concentration. Compared to DAI's direct DAO governance, which empowers broad token holder voting on parameters, Ethena's delegated model facilitates quicker hedging adjustments but diminishes immediate control over operational elements like collateral diversification, appealing to users prioritizing efficiency over pure decentralization.

### 2. Are roles defined?

The OWNER or DEFAULT_ADMIN_ROLE powers include transferring ownership, adding or removing supported collateral assets and custodian addresses, setting the USDe address, granting or revoking MINTER, REDEEMER, and GATEKEEPER roles, and upgrading proxy implementations. These powers are held by the protocol's 7-of-10 multisig wallet requiring multiple signatures. Roles are clearly defined in official documentation, with explicit mappings provided in the key trust assumptions matrix as of October 30, 2025.

#### GATEKEEPER_ROLE
The GATEKEEPER_ROLE powers include disabling minting and redeeming functions immediately and removing MINTER or REDEEMER roles. This role is assigned to the internal Ethena team and external trusted organizations for enhanced security, functioning as an emergency circuit breaker without any delay. A potential maximum loss estimate per incident is approximately $300,000, based on per-block limits of 100,000 USDe, though this carries low confidence. Holders are unspecified trusted organizations, with speculative claims suggesting market makers such as Wintermute, Binance, and Bybit, but these remain low confidence and unverified as of October 30, 2025.

#### MINTER_ROLE
The MINTER_ROLE powers allow minting USDe from assets within 5% of third-party oracle pricing and transferring assets to custodians. This role is granted to specific EOAs controlled by Ethena, with on-chain limitations per block. Estimates suggest approximately 20 EOAs with per-block limits of 100,000 USDe, though this requires verification via hasRole enumeration and carries low confidence as of October 30, 2025.

#### REDEEMER_ROLE
The REDEEMER_ROLE powers allow redeeming USDe for assets within 5% of oracle pricing, similar to the MINTER_ROLE, with on-chain limitations per block.

#### BLACKLISTER_ROLE
The BLACKLISTER_ROLE powers, specific to sUSDe for compliance, allow freezing or blacklisting addresses via the SOFT_RESTRICTED_STAKER_ROLE, which restricts deposit actions such as minting sUSDe from USDe, direct withdrawals through redemption of sUSDe for USDe, and unstaking to receive USDe directly while allowing secondary market transfers, and the FULL_RESTRICTED_STAKER_ROLE, which imposes a complete freeze on all transfers and staking or unstaking operations while enabling fund repossession by the contract owner. This is enforced through hooks like _beforeTokenTransfer() and _beforeCooldown() that revert transactions involving restricted sender or receiver addresses, targeting cases such as restricted jurisdictions like the USA or sanctions and criminal activity. Audit findings highlight potential bypasses for both roles via front-running of blacklist transactions or pre-blacklisting transfers of sUSDe to new addresses, which the protocol views as a best-effort compliance measure and employs Flashbots for private mempool submissions to mitigate, accepting secondary market circumvention given operational constraints. It includes emergency functions like rescueTokens and redistributeLockedAmount. Known blacklist bypass mechanisms from the Code4rena audit dated December 21, 2023, were acknowledged as "by design" by the Ethena team, creating potential compliance risks that rely on operational mitigations like Flashbots rather than strict, unbypassable code enforcement.

The Quantstamp audit completed on October 18, 2023, scoped version 1 including the USDe token and minting/staking architecture, finding no critical or high severity code vulnerabilities, four medium-severity findings, three low-severity findings, and six informational items, noting high trust in off-chain operators, aggregated at docs.ethena.fi/resources/audits and available at quantstamp.com/audits. The Spearbit audit completed on October 18, 2023, covered version 1 protocol contracts and architecture, finding no critical or high severity vulnerabilities, aggregated at docs.ethena.fi/resources/audits. The Pashov audit completed on October 22, 2023, scoped version 1 protocol contracts, finding no critical or high severity vulnerabilities, aggregated at docs.ethena.fi/resources/audits. The Code4rena public contest audit with final report on November 13, 2023, involved a six-day competition from October 24 to October 30, 2023, on version 1 contracts with a $36,500 award pool, finding no critical or high severity vulnerabilities, several valid medium and low-risk findings including blacklist bypass acknowledged as "by design," and gas optimization suggestions, available at code4rena.com/audits/2023-10-ethena-labs. The Pashov Audit Group audit completed on May 23, 2024, reviewed version 2 minting contracts including EthenaMinting and access control from May 20 to May 23, 2024, finding one medium severity vulnerability related to multiple executions due to unsafe uint128 cast in verifyNonce, resolved via safe casting update confirmed in post-audit GitHub code, two low severity issues concerning missing sanity checks during deployment and combining ETH and WETH redemption limits, and no critical or high severity issues, aggregated at docs.ethena.fi/resources/audits. The Chaos Labs economic risk analysis spanned from January 1, 2024, to July 31, 2025, focused on liquid staking tokens, perpetuals, and liquidity risks, publishing multiple reports with no code vulnerabilities identified and modeling tail risks like prolonged negative funding, aggregated at docs.ethena.fi/resources/audits. The Pashov audit completed on September 2, 2024, scoped the sENA contract, finding no critical or high severity vulnerabilities, aggregated at docs.ethena.fi/resources/audits. The Pashov audit completed on October 20, 2024, scoped the USDTB contract, finding no critical or high severity vulnerabilities, aggregated at docs.ethena.fi/resources/audits. The Quantstamp audit completed on October 25, 2024, covered the USDTB token and minting contract from October 23 to October 25, 2024, finding no critical or high severity vulnerabilities, primarily informational and low severity items including recommendations for input validation improvements even for trusted addresses to mitigate human error, documentation enhancements, and code conciseness, all fixed or acknowledged by the Ethena team, available at certificate.quantstamp.com/full/ethena-u-stb-token-and-minting.
